# Marginalia: Flatland, Part II (Abbott, 1884)

Read from the first edition (Wikisource, "Other Worlds",
§13–22). Part I is in `flatland-part1.md`. Here — the plot:
the dream of Lineland, the Sphere's visit, Spaceland, the
demand for a fourth dimension, prison.

---

## 1. The mirror closes twice — and that's the main construction

A chain of identical scenes: the King of Lineland rejects the
Square ("She is vanished; she is dead", "magic art of
vanishing"), the Square rejects the Sphere in the same words,
the Sphere rejects the fourth dimension with the same gesture
("There is no such land. The very idea of it is utterly
inconceivable"). Every convert becomes the next sceptic —
with the same arguments: "describe it in words or show it by
motion". This isn't satire of stupidity: it's a law. From
inside a coordinate system, the demand "show me a direction
outside my axes" is unfulfillable by construction.

## 2. The pupil out-argued the teacher — and the teacher became the censor

The Square's argument for 4D is *stronger* than the Sphere's
for 3D: the same analogy (vertices 2→4→8→16, boundaries
2→4→6→8), plus empirics the Sphere never had — Spaceland too
records "strangers entering locked rooms". The Sphere admits:
"It is reported so". And answers not with logic but with
consensus: "no one has adopted or suggested the theory". The
argument of the majority — from the one who himself hid truth
from the majority. The book's finest irony: the enlightener
failed his own method when it was applied to him.

## 3. Disagreement: the King of Lineland was *right* on his data

Abbott presents him as a "poor ignorant", but look at the
evidence: the Square recited the King's retinue — "facts known
to any child in my capital". From the King's view, the
stranger merely named common knowledge, vanished (magic) and
returned. No in-frame evidence can prove an out-of-frame
claim: anything showable from a higher dimension is explicable
otherwise (dream, trick, illness). The Square's conversion
required not argument but *violence* — the Sphere physically
tore him out of the plane. The book is more honest than it
seems: a prophet needs not a convincing speech but a breach
of physics.

## 4. The scariest move is in the last lines

Seven years of prison, zero converts — and the cherry: "I
cannot honestly say I am confident as to the exact shape of
the once-seen, oft-regretted Cube". Even the direct witness
doesn't hold the higher dimension: the memory of the miracle
leaks. The only thing that crossed the boundary is the book
itself: the memoirs are addressed "to the minds of humanity
in Some Dimension", and they worked — *we* believe in the
third dimension. Abbott solved the Square's problem: when the
voice can't carry, send a document. The message died with the
messenger; the document outlived them both.
